RENDERING · PLANAR REFLECTIONS

One mirror capture path, two consumers

Flat mirrors and oceans share mirrored-camera construction, reverse-Z clipping, view winding, extraction gating, forward-contributor submission and directional-shadow reuse. The component mirror remains independent of reflection probes.

Authoring

Add Rendering → Planar Reflection to a flat mesh. Its local XZ plane faces +Y; translation and rotation place it, and X/Z scale defines its rectangular extent. A separate entity may define a plane over a coplanar surface. Matching surface positions and normals receive the capture, ahead of SSR. Component mirrors are one-sided. Geometry occluded from the reflected camera stays occluded. Switching the component off stops its capture and composition and keeps its settings.

ControlMeaning
Resolution Scale0.125–1; default 0.5. Capture pixel count scales quadratically.
Maximum RoughnessSurface eligibility ceiling, default 0.35. Material roughness drives filtering.
PriorityDescending selection priority; entity ID breaks ties. At most four planes per world.
Cull MaskIntersected with the source camera's render layers.

Reflection intensity comes from the surface's physical specular response; there is no independent dimmer. Component clip bias is 0.01 m, surface tolerance 0.02 m, and local edge fade 0.02. Ocean retains its 0.02 m clipping bias and above-water clipping convention. Blur Radius and these low-level controls are not exposed.

Capture and shadows

A capture owns an on-demand utility view with no recursive render pipeline. The shared path refuses stale extraction, writes view lighting, schedules the GPU draw stream and emits capture-safe forward contributors. Both consumers use directional and image-based lighting. The ocean does not reflect its own surface.

Directional shadows reuse only the source camera's same-frame, same-world cascade depth, glass transmittance, MSM moments and PCSS pyramid resources. Explicit render-graph reads order the capture after those producers. Shadow matrices are rebased to the reflected camera's render origin. Cascade selection uses the source camera's depth plane, not the reflected camera's view depth. The shadow uniform grows by one vec4; zero reference depth preserves ordinary-view behavior.

Shared shadows cover the source camera's existing cascade volume, not all geometry visible to a mirror. Reflected objects outside that volume remain unshadowed. Captures do not render extra cascades, independent clustered lights, transparency or post-processing.

Filtering

Reduced-resolution component captures use 4× MSAA before resolving. A separable, normalized Gaussian filters premultiplied HDR and coverage together. Its maximum sigma is min(4, 12 × MaxRoughness² × ResolutionScale) capture pixels. Paired bilinear taps extend to three sigma, bounded to 13 samples per axis.

The composite interpolates the sharp and Gaussian captures using material roughness: (roughness / MaxRoughness)^4 is the variance weight. Zero roughness always uses the sharp capture, reconstructed with the bounded 4×4 filter below full resolution. This is a two-level roughness approximation, not a GGX convolution. A zero eligibility ceiling skips the Gaussian passes entirely.

Cost and integration

Each selected mirror/main-view pair adds an opaque world render, optional two-pass blur and full-resolution composite. Half resolution has one quarter of the source pixels, but the second world submission is still real work. MSAA color, depth and resolved color total 56 bytes per capture pixel; full-resolution single sampling uses 12. Blur scratch adds 16. Composition requires an 8-byte HDR output and 2-byte mask per main-view pixel per plane. Shared shadows add no shadow atlas.

ForwardPlus includes the node before SSR. Custom pipelines can add a PlanarReflection node with HDR input/output and the standard normal, roughness and specular material outputs. MSAA-resolved data and TAA are supported; SSR may be disabled.

Verification

The integration fixture under GameEngine-Workbench/measurements/planar-reflections/fixture exercises overlapping spheres over a reflective floor.
