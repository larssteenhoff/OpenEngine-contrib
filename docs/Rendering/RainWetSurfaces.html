Rain and wet surfaces

For the shared mirror capture and component controls, see Planar reflections.

Masked puddles

Use Assets/Materials/PuddleGround.material on a flat ground mesh and add a Planar Reflection component at the same height. Set Maximum Roughness to 1 so the material's mask controls the wet boundary without a roughness cutoff. GameEngine-Workbench/agent-scripts/planar-puddles includes a courtyard example with facades, illuminated windows and bollards; its reflective surfaces are flat.

The Surfaces/puddle_ground.glsl shader blends dry rough ground through a damp bank into shallow dielectric water (IOR 1.333, metallic 0). Puddle Coverage and Puddle Size control a stable procedural wetness mask. Dry/wet color, edge width, water roughness and ground grain are exposed in the material inspector. Coverage zero gives dry ground; one wets the whole mesh. Enable Use Mask Texture and assign a grayscale Puddle Mask to use painted shapes: white is wet, black is dry, and gray values form the boundary. This texture is sampled as linear data using mesh UVs and the texture's UV transform.

Surface shaders can set SurfaceOutput.planarReflectionMask independently of opacity. It defaults to 1 for existing mirror materials. The forward pass carries it in NormalRoughness.a, whose old metallic value is no longer needed because SSR uses the exported specular response. The planar composite multiplies its coverage by this mask; no additional attachment is allocated. SSR's material response and normal/roughness channels are unchanged.

This is an opaque shallow-water/ground shading effect, not a water-volume or fluid simulation. The reflected scene is the existing planar capture. The ground stays geometrically flat, and there is no refraction or displaced water surface.

Rain, impacts and runoff

The puddle material also exposes Rain Intensity, Rain Speed, Ripple Strength, Water Normal Strength and Water Normal Size. Small oblique waves provide continuous surface normals between impacts. Rain impacts produce expanding concentric wave normals inside the water mask; the dry road receives softer splash marks that fade continuously over 120 ms. Their filtered coverage conserves brightness at grazing angles. The planar composite uses the wave normal to offset its capture lookup, so the reflected image bends with the ripple. This is a bounded distortion approximation, without refraction or reflected-scene depth tracing.

The rainy courtyard draws falling rain with Shaders/rain_streaks.shaderpkg: 234 compact, camera-facing procedural quads, softly blended after temporal AA and before bloom, depth of field and tonemapping. A 4 mm nominal diameter and 16 ms shutter produce thin streaks from the actual fall speed and camera motion. Subpixel coverage, soft opaque-depth intersections and distance fading keep distant drops light. Additive premultiplied coverage never darkens the scene; only nearby drops take scene-color samples for a small, positive refraction cue. Sky irradiance, directional, ambient and up to 32 punctual lights tint the streaks. The three bollard lamps have warm point lights, and a physical sky supplies the backdrop and environment lighting. Punctual rain lighting does not sample shadow maps. The lighting, shutter and soft-depth choices also follow the production discussion in fxguide’s rain article.

Includes/rain_impacts.glsl provides a shared impact event containing the landing position, cycle, phase and elapsed time for rain, splashes and surface ripples. Falling streaks trail above the leading tip, which reaches the ground at the splash event. All particles start at that same contact point. Ledge cards choose their event from the card center so a card crossing a cell boundary cannot split into different clocks. Match Rain Intensity and Rain Speed between the SoftRain pass, falling-rain and puddle materials to keep them synchronized. Intensity zero disables impacts and drops. Set the ground's Rain Field Min/Max X/Z bounds to the authored rain area. The courtyard uses X [-5.4, 5.4) and Z [-4.2, 3.6), matching its 234 landing cells; it does not generate unmatched shader impacts outside that field. The SoftRain pass's grid origin and dimensions match these same cells. Its proceduralDraw uses six vertices per instance. LightUpload.forwardBuffer publishes the directional/ambient light data it consumes. The post-process auto-parameters supply shaderAnimationTime and frameDeltaTime. FallingRain.material retains crossed cards only for splashes: they are 0.10 m wide and 0.18 m high, centered at each cell's GE_RainCenter, with fallingVisibility zero. This removes the old 8 m strips' refraction overdraw. The stock material still supports full-height cards with Falling Visibility one for other pipelines and authored edge drips.

At each landing, the bottom of the crossed cards renders a burst of five small ballistic splash particles per card. The particles launch upward and outward, fall under gravity, and shrink away within about 0.18–0.32 seconds. They use the completed drop's impact cycle, keeping the burst aligned with the puddle ripple; edge drips use the same effect. Splash Strength adjusts their size and visibility, and zero disables the burst. These shader-driven particles add no emitters, geometry or draw calls, and use the existing thin water refraction path.

Surfaces/rain_wet_object.glsl adds downward wet trails and moving beads to vertical faces, with Surface Color, Surface Roughness, Surface Metallic and Runoff Strength controls. The courtyard materials enable Clear Coat and Coat Normal for a smooth water layer above the rough substrate. The layer uses water IOR 1.333, low roughness and a curved meniscus/bead normal, reflecting both sky and local lights. Its coverage shrinks as the trail dries; uncoated materials use a glossy fallback. EdgeDrips.material uses the same clipped-card shader with accelerating, shorter, thicker drops. The demo places these under window ledges and beside bollard caps. Place drip cards at the object's edge and match Fall Height to the distance to the receiving surface.

This weather demo uses shader animation and authored landing heights. It does not simulate arbitrary collision geometry or automatically find every mesh edge.

Splash cards, fallback falling-rain cards and edge drips use the thin-transmission path on StandardPBR. A shallow droplet normal offsets one post-opaque scene-color sample; Refraction Strength controls the bend. Thick transmission stays disabled, so there is no ray march or two-surface lens tracing. The existing opaque-depth occlusion and screen-edge fallback still apply.

Rain-covered windows

RainWindow.material uses a thin pane with clear transmission in its center. Frosted Border Width controls the soft perimeter, Static Drop Strength controls two jittered layers of stationary refracting beads across the whole pane, and Drop Normal Strength controls larger downward-moving droplets. The courtyard panes are single flat meshes in framed openings, with lit recesses and shelf objects behind them.

Frost uses SurfaceOutput.transmissionBlur, a bounded pixel radius that defaults to zero for existing surfaces. A nonzero radius adds four scene-color taps only at frosted fragments. Splash and edge-drip cards keep the single-tap path. Ripple distortion uses the change in the reflected ray, including its vertical bend, so splash waves remain visible from shallow viewing angles. Surface filtering derives its footprint from continuous world coordinates to avoid seams at impact-cell edges.

Runoff uses independently spaced lanes, segment lengths, widths and slide speeds. Window and wall beads use Includes/rain_runoff.glsl to stick, slide, pause and restart. Each bead varies its two release intervals and pause duration per cycle; smooth acceleration prevents jumps when movement resumes. Trail age comes from the inverse motion curve, so a trail continues to dry while its head is stationary. Drip Drying Time controls how quickly trails narrow and return to the dry material. Both stationary beads and runoff use absolute world surface coordinates in meters; UV layout, camera-facing normals and render-origin rebasing do not reseed them. The fine window layer filters and fades subpixel beads to reduce motion shimmer. Mesh UVs only define the pane’s frosted perimeter. Window frost blurs the transmitted scene without adding a white border. Thin refraction strength defaults to the existing 0.3; clear window regions use 0.025 and wet lenses locally increase it to 0.085. A faint directional variation in transmitted light keeps beads visible against uniform interiors. Rain Intensity controls both stationary bead coverage and the falling window drops.

Shared water controls and wind volumes

Add Rain & Water (RainSettingsEffect) to the global Post Process volume. Water Drop Size is a shared multiplier from 0.25 to 2. Each rain, edge-drip, window, wet-object and puddle material has a Water Drop Size Offset, added before clamping the final size to the same range. Falling Rain Size Offset adjusts the procedural streak pass separately. These controls resize bead footprints and impact rings without changing their world anchors or impact clocks.

Water Ripple Speed animates the water normals on puddles, pane droplets and wall films; zero freezes those travelling waves. Wet-object and window materials also expose Water Ripple Strength and Water Ripple Size. The ground keeps its Water Normal Strength and Water Normal Size controls. Dry substrate normals stay intact.

Wind volumes influence these effects at their world positions, including rotated box, sphere, capsule and cylinder bounds, blend distance, priority and weight. Direction, speed and gusts bend falling rain towards its fixed landing point and affect water ripples. Edge cards keep wind displacement inside their narrow bounds. Rain Amount is a density multiplier: 0 suppresses new rain and impacts, 1 preserves authored density, and 2 doubles it, capped by the field's available drop anchors. Override volumes blend toward their amount; additive volumes add their difference from 1. Existing puddle coverage remains authored separately. The courtyard ships with a global Rain Wind entity so these controls are immediately available.

The weather data shares the forward light uniform buffer and is uploaded once per world/view, with no additional texture reads. Up to 32 enabled wind volumes are sent to water shaders, retaining the highest priorities in deterministic entity order. Materials sample their spatial influence locally; moving the camera does not select a different wind. The forward-light prefix remains unchanged, and its animation-clock update preserves the global size and ripple-speed lanes.

Camera droplets

The courtyard selects RenderPipelines/RainyCourtyard.rendergraph, a ForwardPlus variant with an optional CameraRain fullscreen pass after antialiasing. Shaders/camera_rain.frag scatters small lens droplets in two size layers; they appear, rest, occasionally slide down, and dry away. It samples scene color once at a bent UV without darkening or whitening the image. lensRainIntensity sets coverage (zero clears the lens), and lensRefraction sets the bend in pixels. The courtyard disables and bypasses this pass so camera-fixed lenses do not distort window frames or resemble oversized droplets on nearby surfaces. To opt in, enable CameraRain and connect FinalCopy to LDRAfterLensRain. The pass receives the engine shader clock through shaderAnimationTime. Disable the pass to remove its cost. This camera effect applies to the rainy pipeline; the standard ForwardPlus pipeline keeps its existing behavior.

Open the courtyard project and choose its RainyCourtyard pipeline as the active render pipeline in the editor. The game's configuration also selects this pipeline.
